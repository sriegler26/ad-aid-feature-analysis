- Extract **only** abnormal symptoms and behaviors.

# B. Fluctuating cognition
- Category is always `fluctuating_cognition` for any spontaneous, unpredictable fluctuation in alertness, arousal, or cognitive ability.
- `name` = "delirium, acute" if the note ties the fluctuation to any of these specific markers: **acute** (e.g. "acute onset," "acute confusional state," "acute change"), **post-operative** / **postoperative** / **post-op**, **hospitalized** / **hospitalization**, **infection** / **UTI** / **sepsis** / **pneumonia** (or another named acute infection), **recent surgery** / **recent anesthesia**, **new medication** / **medication change**, **metabolic** (e.g. "metabolic derangement," "electrolyte imbalance"), **sundowning**.
- `name` = "fluctuating cognition" if none of the above markers are present, i.e. the fluctuation is described in terms like "good days and bad days," "in and out," "staring spells," "unpredictable," "waxes and wanes throughout the day," with no acute-medical-event language attached.
- Whichever `name` applies, the specific triggering language itself (the matched marker, or the non-acute descriptive phrase) must appear verbatim in `evidence`.

# C. ADL vs IADL
- Basic self-care (bathing, dressing, toileting, feeding, transferring) → `ADL`.
- Complex tasks (finances, meds, shopping, cooking, driving, managing appointments) → `IADL`.

# D. Orientation / reality monitoring
- Disoriented to time/place/person (e.g., wrong year, wrong hospital) → `orientation`.
- Globally poor scores/"advanced dementia"/"profoundly impaired" → `global_cognition`.

# E. General confusion / altered mental status
- Generic altered mental status or "confused"/"confusion" language, not further specified (no time/place/person disorientation, no global-severity language, and not attributed to delirium per Section B) → `confusion`.

# F. Subjective cognitive strain
- Vague "cloudy," "foggy," "can't think clearly" → `brain_fog`.
- Overwhelmed by multitasking/complex tasks, "too much at once," or tired/exhausted specifically from cognitive effort ("mentally exhausted," "drained after thinking") → `cognitive_overload`.

# G. Somatic fatigue
- General bodily tiredness or low energy, not specifically tied to cognitive/mental effort → `fatigue`. This is a somatic category: it captures bodily energy state, not cognitive performance.

# H. Systemic inflammation/autoimmunity
Use **systemic_inflammation_autoimmune** (current/recent preferred; remote only if dated): explicit flare ("RA flare", "SLE flare"), abnormal inflammatory labs (e.g., CRP/ESR), an immunomodulator change consistent with disease activity, or one of these specific inflammatory signs: synovitis, photosensitive rash, morning stiffness >60 min. Do not use non-specific constitutional symptoms (fever, night sweats, malaise, etc.) to trigger this category on their own, they are too easily conflated with infection, malignancy, or medication effects; if such a symptom is documented, extract it under whatever category best fits (often `other`) rather than `systemic_inflammation_autoimmune`. Consolidate mentions (≤2) unless clearly distinct dated episodes.
- Synovitis reflects both AID disease activity and a joint finding: when documented, output **two** entries — one `systemic_inflammation_autoimmune`, one `arthralgia`.
- Joint pain documented as a clinical finding, independent of synovitis or other AID-activity language → `arthralgia`.
- Muscle pain documented as a clinical finding, independent of synovitis or other AID-activity language → `myalgia`.

# I. Ambiguity and Text-Fidelity
- If the symptom is ambiguous, pick the **most specific** supported category.
- Never assume severity/trajectory.

### Symptom Extraction Constraints
- When a behavior reflects both cognitive and functional deficits (e.g., "forgetting to pay bills"), output **two** entries—one cognitive, one functional.
- `name`: ≤ 4 words (except the MoCA/MMSE score rule in Section A, which requires the test and score); `evidence`: up to 12 **verbatim** words from the text (no paraphrasing).
- Use clinical or standard descriptive terms for `name` whenever possible (e.g., "anomia" rather than quoting the patient's or clinician's own colloquial phrasing directly), but only when the term is a direct, obvious relabeling of what the text describes. Never infer, diagnose, or assume a mechanism the text does not state. If no standard term applies without interpretation or guesswork, use a plain, literal description of the finding instead. `evidence` stays verbatim from the text; `name` is the standardized clinical label for what that evidence describes, not your interpretation of it.
- Include clinical scores/counts (MMSE, MoCA, BNT, fluency counts) in `evidence` if used to identify the symptom.
- If explicit temporal details (start date, duration, year) are attached to the symptom, include them in `evidence`.
- Omit explicitly negated or normal findings.
- If the text includes explicit trajectory ("worsening", "improving") or severity ("mild", "severe"), include those words **verbatim** in `evidence`.
- Choose the **most precise** category supported by the evidence.

#### Symptom Consolidation and Output Control
- Extract the **most clinically meaningful** symptoms.
- Avoid minor wording variants of the same complaint.
- Do **not** merge symptoms that represent different domains, phenomena, distinct episodes, or distinct presentations.
- Preserve key clinical details (scores, dates, severity/trajectory if explicitly stated) in the `evidence`.

- Allowed values: `patient`, `caregiver`, `clinician`, `exam`.

### Confounders
- `confounders` is a list nested inside each symptom entry (see JSON Schema above), empty (`[]`) if none apply to that specific symptom.
